## 1. Title

*Did UK recessions give warning in GDP data as first released? A registered extension (E4) using real-time vintages of UK GDP.*

## 2. Question and hypothesis

H1 uses one late vintage, published long after most of the quarters it covers. E4 asks whether the registered pre-onset change in the rolling AR(2) root modulus appears in the data as first released. For each H1 episode, Δr is computed from the **first vintage containing the quarter before onset**. Two things are asked:

- whether the mean real-time change S_rt is unusually large relative to fixed-date surrogates generated from each vintage;
- descriptively, how the real-time Δr compares with the final-data Δr.

**Hypothesis E4 (one-sided).** S_rt (§8) is unusually large relative to the §9 surrogate distribution. The alternative is upper-tailed, and there is one confirmatory comparison, at W = 40.

**Two clocks (SD-07), disclosed.** The first vintage containing t_r − 1 is typically released after t_r − 1 ends, so possibly during the onset quarter t_r. ONS documentation describes first quarterly estimates as released around six weeks after the end of the reference quarter and the quarterly national accounts around 12 to 13 weeks after it (GDP quality and methodology report and National Accounts Revisions Policy, read 29 September 2026; Annex C; also `audit/STATISTICAL_DESIGN.md` SD-07). E4 is therefore a test of the **revision sensitivity** of the H1 statistic, measured in the first vintage that contains the quarter before onset. In that vintage t_r − 1 appears for the first time in the acquired workbook, which is its first release if the workbook holds a vintage for every release (§7, step 2); every earlier quarter stands as revised up to that vintage, so the vintage series is not a series of first estimates. E4 is **not** a test of warning available before the onset quarter began. The onset dates themselves come from final data and remain a retrospective reference.

## 5. Variables

For vintage v and episode j with onset quarter q_j, the vintage series is defined as follows.

- **Levels.** Take the levels in vintage v for the contiguous run of present quarters ending at **q_j − 1**. Start at the later of **1955Q1** and the first quarter of that run. A quarter before q_j − 1 with no level ends the run: the run starts at the quarter after it, and the length rule (§7, step 3) then decides. Quarters after q_j − 1, if any, are ignored.
- **Growth.** `g_v[t] = 400*(ln(Y_v[t]) - ln(Y_v[t-1]))`, at positions 0,…,n_v−1, where position n_v − 1 is q_j − 1.
- **Stop.** A level in the run that is non-positive or non-finite makes the episode **unavailable**, with the reason recorded. Nothing is interpolated.
- **Rebasing.** Each vintage series is taken from a single vintage and is never spliced across vintages, so differences between vintages in reference year, price basis or concept do not enter any one series. They are recorded from the workbook's notes and disclosed, and are not adjusted.

## 6. Model and indicator

H1 §4 unchanged: a rolling OLS AR(2) with intercept, **W = 40**, and M(t) from the companion roots, computed separately within each vintage series. The first indicator is at position 39 of each vintage series.

## 7. Episodes, vintages and eligibility

For each H1 eligible episode j, in chronological onset order (j = 0 for 1973Q3, 1 for 1980Q1, 2 for 1990Q3 and 3 for 2008Q2; j is not renumbered when an episode is unavailable):

1. **First vintage.** v_j is the earliest vintage in which a level for q_j − 1 is present.
2. **First-release check.** The vintage immediately before v_j must exist, and must have no level for q_j − 1. If v_j is the earliest vintage in the acquired workbook, the episode is **unavailable (first release unverifiable)**. The check establishes that v_j is the first vintage in the acquired workbook with a level for q_j − 1; it establishes the first publication of an estimate for q_j − 1 only if the workbook holds a vintage for every release, which is not documented (Annex C).
3. **Length.** The vintage series (§5) must have n_v ≥ **48** growth observations, so that M(n_v − 9) exists at W = 40.
4. **Availability.** An episode satisfying 1–3 whose vintage series has no §5 stop is **E4-eligible**. All others are reported with the step that failed. A failed rolling fit, or a non-finite M_v at a position that Delta_rt requires, in the series of an E4-eligible episode is a failure of the observed statistic (H1 §§4–5): it fails the comparison and does not make the episode unavailable.

Let m_E4 be the number of E4-eligible episodes. If m_E4 = 0, E4 is **not estimable**. It is recorded as failed and enters DR-2 with Holm input 1 (H1 §11).

*Expected consequence (unverified).* If the workbook's earliest vintage is later than some H1 onsets, those episodes are unavailable under step 1 or 2. The rule fixes this outcome in advance. It is not a choice made after seeing coverage.

## 8. Statistic

For each E4-eligible episode j, with the onset at position n_v (one past the last quarter of the vintage series, q_j − 1):

`Delta_rt[j] = M_v(n_v - 1) - M_v(n_v - 9)`

Then `S_rt = mean(Delta_rt)` over the m_E4 episodes, and `k = count(Delta_rt > 0)`. Record every vintage selection, n_v and each signed Delta_rt.

## 9. Null and p-value (fixed-date surrogates per vintage)

**Per episode j, from its own vintage series.**

- Fit the intercept-inclusive OLS AR(2) to all n_v growth values, using n_v − 2 rows.
- Require **strict stability** as H1 §6. An unstable or failed null for **any** E4-eligible episode fails the comparison before any draw (fail-closed, as H1; D-023, E4-4).
- Centre the residuals.

**Draws.** For each attempt b = 0,…,**999**, and for each episode j:

- draw n_v − 2 residual indices in one `integers(0, n_v-2, size=n_v-2)` call from the episode's own generator;
- regenerate the vintage series from its first two observed values with its fitted null;
- compute `Delta_b[j] = M(n_v - 1) - M(n_v - 9)` at the **same fixed positions**, with no recession detection.

Then `S_b = mean_j Delta_b[j]`.

**p-value.**

- Every attempt is structurally eligible, so B′ = 1,000 whenever the p-value is valid.
- A failure in any episode's attempt b is recorded and invalidates the p-value, as H1 §6.
- `p = (1+K)/(B'+1)`, with K = count(S_b ≥ S_rt). Report K, B′, q and the Wilson interval as in H1 §6.

**Decision rule DR-E4.** Reject when the **Holm-adjusted p ≤ 0.05** (§10). Report the raw p at freeze, labelled "raw, not family-adjusted".

**Random numbers.** As H1 §8, with master seed **1927**.

- Episode j uses generator `SeedSequence([1927, 5400, j, 0])`, drawing attempts in numerical order.
- Episodes do not share a generator, so one episode's failure cannot shift another's draws.

## 11. Outcome rule and the X.3 synthetic design

**Outcome.** As H1 §10, with the Holm-adjusted p in place of the raw p: adjusted p ≤ 0.05 gives the "unusually large" statement with S_rt, every Delta_rt, k/m and raw and adjusted p; adjusted p > 0.05 is inconclusive, with the Branch B diagnostic reported as a labelled diagnostic; an undefined p or failure is not estimable or failed. The reference is "fixed-date AR(2) surrogates fitted to each episode's first vintage containing the quarter before onset". Wording must keep SD-07's limit: revision sensitivity, not pre-onset availability.

**X.3 synthetic design.** These are the AT-15 and AT-16 checks adapted to E4. They are run after registration and before any real-time level is read. None has been run.

- **Generating process.** H1 §9 exactly: 259 observations, a = 0.3, b = 0.1, c = 1.5, s.d. 3.5, stationary initial pair, no burn-in.
- **Synthetic vintages.** For each series, the five H1 imposed onsets at zero-based positions **49, 99, 149, 199, 249** each define a "vintage": positions 0,…,r−1 of the same series, so n_v = r. There is no revision noise. The check measures the per-vintage fixed-date procedure, not the effect of revisions. This limit is disclosed.
- **Size (AT-15 adapted).**
  - 200 base series from stream 5420 (cell 0, replicates 0–199).
  - The E4 procedure (§§8–9) at the five synthetic vintages, with surrogates from stream 5421, cell = j, here the synthetic vintage (0–4), replicate = series index.
  - Pass: rejection rate between **0.02 and 0.09** inclusive, with all 200 valid. A rejection in the cells is a valid raw p ≤ 0.05, as in H1 §9.
- **Power (AT-16 adapted).**
  - κ ∈ {1.0, 1.2, 1.4, 1.6}, **200** series each. Generation uses stream 5430 (cell = κ index 0–3, replicate 0–199).
  - H1's planted `(kappa*0.3, kappa^2*0.1)` coefficients and mean-preserving intercept go in the eight positions before each of the five onsets.
  - The E4 procedure runs on the five truncations, with surrogates from stream 5431, cell = 10·(κ index) + j, replicate = series index.
- **Reporting and D80.** As H1 §9, including the 1.96-SE decrease flag and the first-raw-crossing D80.
- **Expected consequence (unverified).** H1's registered power cells reached a rejection rate of 0.235 at κ = 1.6, so H1's D80 is undefined (D-032). E4 has at most four episodes and its cells use five synthetic vintages, so its power on the real vintages may be lower than the cells show, and its D80 may be undefined; the Branch B diagnostic would then be unavailable. This follows from the design and is recorded, not corrected.
