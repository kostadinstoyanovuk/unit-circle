## 1. Title

*Did UK recessions give warning over three centuries? A registered extension (E1) of a test of critical slowing down, using annual real GDP, 1700–2016.*

## 2. Question and hypothesis

E1 asks the H1 question (H1 §2) of a longer and coarser record. Before the onsets of UK annual real GDP declines from 1733 to 2016, did the largest companion-root modulus M(t) of a rolling AR(2) fitted to annual growth rise more than the registered fitted constant-parameter AR(2) surrogate procedure would produce?

**Hypothesis E1 (one-sided).** The mean pre-onset change S (§8) is unusually large relative to the surrogate distribution in §9. The alternative is upper-tailed. There is one confirmatory comparison, at W = 30. As in H1, its interpretation depends on the specified model, the fitted nuisance parameters and the calibration. A rejection is not evidence that uniquely identifies critical slowing down (H1 §10; research-repo D-007).

## 3. Prior work and difference

1. The registered H1 background (H1 §2, lines 1–5) applies unchanged: Rye and Jackson (2020), *Scientific Reports* 10, 10481, is documented related macroeconomic work using lag-one autocorrelation and variance on historical GDP datasets, with a focus on variability over multiple decades.
2. E1 differs from H1 only in data frequency, span and the rules the plan changes: annual data, a two-year horizon, single-year recessions and war-year exclusions.
3. The plan's Paper I literature search (plan §11, p. 12) had not been completed at registration. Nothing here claims how E1 relates to work not yet searched. This is not a claim of novelty.
4. Short-window least-squares AR(2) estimates carry O(1/n) bias (Shaman and Stine 1988, *JASA* 83, 842–848, Table 1, p. 846). This is as summarised in the programme's Paper II literature review (plan C3.L); the paper was not re-read for this addendum. With W = 30 each fit has 28 regression rows, against 38 in H1. The surrogate procedure refits the same estimator, so the bias enters observed and surrogate statistics alike, but it is disclosed as a limitation.

## 5. Variables

`g[y] = 100*(ln(Y[y]) - ln(Y[y-1]))` for y = 1701,…,2016: **316** annual percentage growth observations.

- Index them by zero-based position i = 0,…,315, with year(i) = 1701 + i.
- No level before 1700 is used.
- No seasonal adjustment, detrending or outlier treatment is applied.

## 6. Model and indicator

As H1 §4, with W replaced by **W = 30**.

- At each position t ≥ 29, fit `g[s] = c + phi1*g[s-1] + phi2*g[s-2] + error[s]` by ordinary least squares with an intercept to the 30 observations g[t−29],…,g[t]. The first two serve only as lags, giving **28 regression rows**.
- The numerical method, rank and finiteness requirements, root formulas and M(t) = max(|λ1|, |λ2|) are exactly as H1 §4.
- The first indicator is at position 29 (year 1730).
- Estimates outside the stationary region are kept; nothing is clipped or projected.
- Sensitivity windows: W = 25 (first indicator at position 24) and W = 35 (position 34).

## 7. Recessions

**Rule.** A qualifying recession run is **one or more consecutive years with g[i] < 0**. Zero or positive growth ends a run. The onset is the first year of the run and the end is its last year, both inclusive. A terminal run qualifies.

**Merging.** Process runs chronologically. If a run's onset position minus the preceding episode's inclusive end position is **≤ 2**, merge it into that episode: keep the episode's onset and extend its end to the new run's end. Merges may chain. Merge before eligibility and before the exogenous classification below.

For example, runs {i} and {i+2} merge (difference 2); runs {i} and {i+3} do not.

**Exogenous-shock episodes.** An episode whose onset year is in 1914–1918, 1939–1945 or 2020 (inclusive) is **exogenous**. This is decided by the onset year only, after merging.

- An exogenous episode is excluded from S and from every confirmatory and secondary statistic.
- Its Δr, where computable, is reported in a separate descriptive table.
- A later run merged into an exogenous episode is part of that episode and is excluded with it.
- Onset year 2020 lies outside the sample (§4). That clause is kept because the plan registers it, and it cannot apply.

**Eligibility.** A non-exogenous episode is eligible if M(r−3) exists and M(r−1) and M(r−3) are finite. For W = 30 the earliest eligible onset position is **32** (year 1733). For W = 25 it is 27 (1728); for W = 35 it is 37 (1738). An episode whose onset is ineligible stays ineligible even if a run merged into it would have been eligible alone (H1 §5). Earlier recessions may lie inside the windows.

## 8. Statistic

For each eligible episode with onset position r:

`Delta[r] = M(r-1) - M(r-3)`

Both windows end before the onset. For W = 30 they cover r−32,…,r−3 and r−30,…,r−1 and share 28 observations.

Let m be the number of eligible episodes, `S = sum(Delta[r])/m` and `k = count(Delta > 0)`.

- Record every qualifying run, every merged episode and its classification (exogenous, eligible, ineligible), each signed Delta, m, S and k.
- k is descriptive.
- If m = 0, S and p are not estimable: no null fit and no draws, as in H1 §5.

## 9. Null and p-value

As H1 §6, with these replacements:

- Fit the intercept-inclusive OLS AR(2) once to all **316** growth observations (**314** conditional regression rows). War years are included in the fit.
- Require strict stability, as in H1 §6. There is no projection.
- Centre the 314 residuals by their arithmetic mean.
- For each of exactly **B = 1,000 attempted** draws, sample 314 residual indices in one `integers(0,314,size=314)` call. Generate positions 2,…,315 recursively from the observed g[0], g[1] with the fitted intercept and coefficients.
- **Endogenous onsets (primary).** In every surrogate, refit every W = 30 window and apply §7 in full: runs, merging, the exogenous classification by year(i) = 1701 + i, and eligibility. Then compute S_b.
- Drop and count a draw only when it has no eligible non-exogenous episode. Failures are recorded and invalidate the comparison exactly as H1 §6. The identity attempted = retained + no_eligible + failed holds.
- Raw p-value: `p = (1+K)/(B'+1)`, with K = count(S_b ≥ S) counting ties and B′ the number of retained draws. If B′ = 0, p is undefined.
- Also report B′, K, the grid spacing, q = K/B′ and its 95% Wilson interval, as in H1 §6.
- **Decision rule DR-E1.** The E1 comparison rejects when its **Holm-adjusted p ≤ 0.05** (§10). The raw p is reported when E1 is frozen, labelled "raw, not family-adjusted". No rejection is declared from raw p alone.

**Random numbers.** NumPy `Generator(PCG64(SeedSequence([1927, stream_id, cell, replicate])))`, with the stream ids in Annex A. Master seed **1927**, as in H1 §8. All H1 §8 conventions apply: attempts in numerical order, all innovations for an attempt drawn before its recursion, no randomness consumed by fits or onset detection, and generator states recorded before and after each analysis.

## 10. Secondary analyses and the extension family

**Episode interval.** As H1 §7: 10,000 resamples of the m eligible Delta values in one `integers(0,m,size=(10000,m))` call from stream 5105. The 5th and 95th percentiles use NumPy's linear quantile convention.

**Secondary comparisons.** Each requests 1,000 attempts, uses the same upper-tail p and failure accounting, and is reported in one table even when it disagrees with the confirmatory comparison. None replaces it.

| Analysis | Exact specification |
|---|---|
| W = 25 and W = 35 | Repeat the complete E1 procedure (§§6–9) at each window with window-specific eligibility and independent surrogate streams 5101 and 5102 |
| Fixed observed dates | W = 30. Use the observed eligible non-exogenous onset positions unchanged in every surrogate; do not detect surrogate recessions. Stream 5103 |
| Wild signs | W = 30, endogenous onsets. Keep the centred residuals in their original order and multiply each by an independent equiprobable sign: one `integers(0,2,size=314)` call per draw, 0 → −1 and 1 → +1. Stream 5104 |
| Kendall trend | For each eligible episode, tau-b between positions 0,1,2,3 and M(r−4),…,M(r−1); average the episode values. Requires M(r−4) to exist, so the earliest onset at W = 30 is position 33. With 4 values there are 6 pairs; the denominator is `sqrt(6*(6-T))`, where T counts tied-modulus pairs, and T = 6 is an undefined-statistic failure. Otherwise as H1 §7 |
| Lag-one comparator | In each W = 30 growth window, A(t) as H1 §7. Form A(r−1) − A(r−3), average over the §7 eligible episodes and report its own upper-tail p |

The same 1,000 primary surrogate paths (stream 5100) supply the primary, Kendall and lag-one comparisons, with statistic-specific eligibility and retention as in H1 §7.

**Descriptive only (no test).**

- The exogenous episodes' Δr at W = 30.
- The territory stretch in which each eligible episode's two windows lie. An episode whose windows cross a territory boundary is flagged, not excluded.

**Extension family (DR-2), as registered in H1 §11.**

- The family is E1–E4. H1 is not a member and remains the single primary test.
- E1's raw p is reported when E1 is frozen.
- The family closes when every member has been frozen, recorded as failed, or dropped. A dated `DECISIONS.md` entry records the closure.
- At closure, apply Holm at family-wise α = 0.05 to the members:
  - a registered extension never run, or registered and failed, enters with Holm input 1, and its raw p stays missing;
  - an extension dropped before its addendum leaves the family, logged in `DEVIATIONS.md`.
- For h members, order the inputs increasingly, breaking ties by extension number. The adjusted value at rank i is `min(1, max_{j≤i}((h−j+1)·p_(j)))`.

## 11. Outcome rule and the X.3 synthetic design

**Outcome (as H1 §10, with the Holm-adjusted p of §10).**

- Adjusted p ≤ 0.05: "The observed mean pre-onset change in annual data was unusually large relative to the registered fitted constant-AR(2) surrogate procedure." Report S, its sign, every Delta, k/m, raw and adjusted p and all counts. If S ≤ 0, say so.
- Adjusted p > 0.05: inconclusive. "The E1 comparison did not detect an unusually large pre-onset change under the registered surrogate model." Also report the H1 Branch B diagnostic: adjusted p > 0.05, D80 defined, and the upper end of the 90% episode interval strictly below D80. It is labelled a diagnostic, not a finding of absence.
- Undefined p or failure: not estimable or failed, with reasons.

**X.3 synthetic design.** These are the AT-15 and AT-16 checks adapted to E1. They are run after registration and before any E1 value is read, without tuning the pipeline. None has been run.

- **Generating process.** Each series has **316** observations, one per position i with year(i) = 1701 + i.
  - Base recursion as H1 §9: a = 0.3, b = 0.1, c = 1.5, mean 2.5, Gaussian innovations with s.d. **3.5**, roots 0.5 and −0.2, no burn-in.
  - The parameters are kept from H1 because the plan's E1 row changes none of them. The test is invariant to affine rescaling of the series except through the sign rule, so only mean/s.d. matters.
  - Initial pair from the base stationary distribution: v = 1225/88, h = v/3, one `standard_normal(2)` call, as H1 §9.
  - The remaining 314 innovations come from one `normal(0,3.5,size=314)` call.
- **Size (AT-15 adapted).**
  - 200 base series, generated from stream 5120 (cell 0, replicates 0–199), with surrogates from stream 5121 (matching replicate).
  - Each is analysed in the E1 primary mode: W = 30, §7 rule with merging ≤ 2, the exogenous classification by synthetic year, eligibility from position 32, and 1,000 endogenous-onset residual surrogates.
  - Pass: an empirical rejection rate (raw p ≤ 0.05) between **0.02 and 0.09 inclusive**, with all 200 replicates valid. Failure accounting and uncertainty reporting follow H1 §9.
- **Power (AT-16 adapted).**
  - κ ∈ {1.0, 1.2, 1.4, 1.6}, **200** series each. Generation uses stream 5130 (cells 0–3 in κ order, replicates 0–199); fixed-onset surrogates use stream 5131 (matching cell and replicate).
  - **Seven** imposed onsets at one-based positions **35, 75, 115, 155, 195, 235, 275**, which are zero-based **34, 74, 114, 154, 194, 234, 274** (synthetic years 1735, 1775, 1815, 1855, 1895, 1935, 1975).
  - Why these positions: the spacing of 40 exceeds the 32 years a W = 30 window reaches back from M(r−3), so no window reaches the previous signal; every onset is eligible; and none falls in an exogenous year.
  - In the **two** positions r−2 and r−1 before each onset, replace the coefficients by `(kappa*0.3, kappa^2*0.1)` and the intercept by `2.5*(1-kappa*0.3-kappa^2*0.1)`. Everywhere else, including onsets, use the base values. The planted largest modulus is 0.5, 0.6, 0.7 and 0.8.
  - Compute S at the seven imposed positions with W = 30, and surrogate S at the same positions (fixed-date proxy, H1 §9).
  - Report every cell as H1 §9: rates, Wilson intervals, adjacent differences with the 1.96-SE decrease flag, mean S and its standard error.
- **D80.** As H1 §9: the first raw crossing of 0.80, interpolated linearly in κ; undefined if any cell is invalid or no κ ≤ 1.6 reaches 0.80.
- **Expected consequence (unverified).** Only 2 of the 30 observations in the M(r−1) window carry the planted signal, against 8 of 40 in H1, so power at κ ≤ 1.6 may be low and D80 may be undefined. If so, the Branch B diagnostic is unavailable. This follows from the plan's two-year horizon and is not a reason to change the design after seeing X.3 results (D-023, E1-5).
