**Title:** E4: Did UK recessions give warning in GDP data as first released? A registered extension of OSF wcnbz using real-time vintages of UK GDP (ABMI)

**Description:** This registration is extension E4 of the registered H1 protocol, OSF wcnbz (DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/WCNBZ), and a member of its extension family E1-E4 (with E1, OSF mjg9w; E2, not yet registered; and E3, OSF rhzsm). For each H1 eligible episode (onsets 1973Q3, 1980Q1, 1990Q3 and 2008Q2, from the frozen H1 record), it computes the pre-onset change in the largest companion-root modulus of a rolling AR(2) of quarterly GDP growth from the first vintage, in one edition of the ONS real-time database of GDP levels (ABMI), that contains the quarter before the onset. It tests whether the mean change is unusually large relative to fixed-date surrogates from a constant-parameter AR(2) fitted to each episode's vintage series, with a 40-quarter primary window and 1,000 attempted surrogates per episode. It is a test of the revision sensitivity of the H1 statistic, not of warning available before the onset quarter began. The attached addendum states only its replacements of the H1 rules; every other H1 rule applies unchanged. The decision uses Holm-adjusted p-values within E1-E4. The database's coverage is not documented on the ONS pages read; the addendum is registered without that documentation, its rules settle every coverage outcome in advance, and poor coverage could leave E4 with few or no usable episodes. Registration precedes acquisition of the real-time data; the results of H1 and E3 are known at registration. The study does not claim causal tipping, real-time warning or forecasting, or established novelty.

## Study information

### Research questions [72-2]

For the H1 eligible primary episodes (onsets 1973Q3, 1980Q1, 1990Q3 and 2008Q2 in the frozen H1 record) that meet the availability rules of E4 section 7, is the mean pre-onset change in the largest companion-root modulus of a rolling AR(2) with window W=40 unusually large relative to fixed-date AR(2) surrogates generated from each episode's vintage series, when each episode's change is computed from the first vintage, in the acquired workbook of the ONS real-time database (ABMI levels), that contains the quarter before the onset? Descriptively, how does each real-time change compare with the final-data change of H1, and how do the release dates of the vintages relate to the onset quarter (the two clocks)? E4 is a test of the revision sensitivity of the H1 statistic, measured in the first vintage that contains the quarter before onset. In that vintage the quarter before onset appears for the first time in the acquired workbook, which is its first release if the workbook holds a vintage for every release; every earlier quarter stands as revised up to that vintage, so the vintage series is not a series of first estimates. The first-release check of section 7 establishes the first publication of an estimate for the quarter before onset only if the workbook holds a vintage for every release, which is not documented. E4 is not a test of warning available before the onset quarter began: the first vintage containing the quarter before onset is typically released after that quarter ends, so possibly during the onset quarter, and the onset dates come from final data and remain a retrospective reference. The prespecified secondary comparisons (window length, wild signs, Kendall trend, lag-one comparator) do not create additional primary hypotheses. This is extension E4 of H1 (OSF wcnbz). See E4 sections 2 and 7-10.

### Hypotheses [72-4]

E4 is directional and upper-tailed, with one confirmatory comparison at W=40. Compare the observed mean signed real-time change S_rt over the E4-eligible episodes with the distribution of the surrogate means S_b from 1,000 attempted fixed-date surrogate draws, in which each episode's vintage series is regenerated from the constant-parameter AR(2) fitted to it and the change is taken at the same fixed positions, with no recession detection. E4 rejects only when its Holm-adjusted p<=0.05 within the extension family E1-E4, computed at family closure; its raw p is reported when E4 is frozen, labelled "raw, not family-adjusted". As in H1, this is a model-conditional comparison; rejection alone does not establish an absolute positive rise in persistence. The hypothesis concerns the revision sensitivity of the H1 statistic, measured in the first vintage that contains the quarter before onset; it is not a test of warning available before the onset quarter began. If no episode is E4-eligible, E4 is not estimable, is recorded as failed and enters the family with Holm input 1. See E4 sections 2, 7 and 9-11.

### Measured variables [72-33]

Inputs are the levels Y_v[t] of UK real GDP in each real-time vintage v (chained volume measures at market prices) and the frozen H1 record. The episodes are indexed j=0 for 1973Q3, 1 for 1980Q1, 2 for 1990Q3 and 3 for 2008Q2; j is not renumbered when an episode is unavailable. For vintage v and episode j with onset quarter q_j, take the levels in vintage v for the contiguous run of present quarters (numeric cells) ending at q_j-1, starting at the later of 1955Q1 and the first quarter of that run; a quarter before q_j-1 with no level ends the run, the run starts at the quarter after it, and the length rule then decides; quarters after q_j-1 are ignored. Growth is g_v[t]=400*(ln(Y_v[t])-ln(Y_v[t-1])) at positions 0,...,n_v-1, where position n_v-1 is q_j-1. In each 40-observation window, fit an intercept-inclusive AR(2) by OLS using its final 38 observations as responses (H1 section 4, computed separately within each vintage series); M(t) is the largest modulus of the companion roots, and the first indicator is at position 39 of each vintage series. The first vintage v_j is the earliest vintage in which a level for q_j-1 is present; the vintage immediately before v_j must exist and have no level for q_j-1, and if v_j is the earliest vintage in the acquired workbook the episode is unavailable (first release unverifiable). The vintage series must have n_v>=48 growth observations. For each E4-eligible episode, Delta_rt[j]=M_v(n_v-1)-M_v(n_v-9); S_rt is the mean of the signed Delta_rt values over the m_E4 E4-eligible episodes, and k is the number of positive Delta_rt. Descriptive variables: Delta_final from the frozen H1 record and its difference from Delta_rt, and the two-clock quantities listed in [72-59]. There are no additional covariates. Secondary variables are defined exactly in E4 section 10.

## Analyses

### Statistical models [72-49]

Use the OLS AR(2), companion-root and window definitions of H1 section 4 within each episode's vintage series (E4 sections 5-8). For each E4-eligible episode j, fit an intercept-inclusive OLS AR(2) to all n_v growth values of its vintage series (n_v-2 rows) and require strict stability as in H1 section 6; an unstable or failed null for any E4-eligible episode fails the comparison before any draw (fail-closed). Centre the residuals. For each attempt b=0,...,999 and each episode j, draw n_v-2 residual indices in one integers(0,n_v-2,size=n_v-2) call from the episode's own generator, regenerate the vintage series from its first two observed values with its fitted null, and compute Delta_b[j]=M(n_v-1)-M(n_v-9) at the same fixed positions, with no recession detection; S_b is the mean of Delta_b[j] over the episodes. Every attempt is structurally eligible, so B'=1,000 whenever the p-value is valid; a failure in any episode's attempt b is recorded and invalidates the p-value, as in H1 section 6. With K the number of S_b>=S_rt, ties included, the raw p=(1+K)/(B'+1); report K, B', q and the Wilson interval as in H1 section 6. Master seed 1927 with PCG64: episode j uses SeedSequence([1927, 5400, j, 0]), drawing attempts in numerical order, and episodes do not share a generator, so one episode's failure cannot shift another's draws. The streams used are 5400, 5401, 5402, 5404, 5405, 5420, 5421, 5430 and 5431, within the range 5400-5431 (Annex A); 5403 is deliberately unused, because the confirmatory comparison is already fixed-date. See E4 sections 6-9 and Annex A.

### Effect size [72-51]

### Inference criteria [72-55]

One upper-tailed confirmatory comparison at W=40 (decision rule DR-E4). E4 rejects only when its Holm-adjusted p<=0.05 within the extension family E1-E4 at family-wise alpha 0.05, computed at family closure. The family closes when every member has been frozen, recorded as failed, or dropped; a registered extension never run, or registered and failed, enters with Holm input 1 and its raw p stays missing; an extension dropped before its addendum leaves the family. For h members the inputs are ordered increasingly, ties broken by extension number, and the adjusted value at rank i is min(1, max over j<=i of (h-j+1)*p_(j)). The raw p is reported at freeze, labelled "raw, not family-adjusted". The outcome follows H1 section 10 with the adjusted p in place of the raw p: adjusted p<=0.05 gives the "unusually large" statement, with S_rt, every Delta_rt, k/m and the raw and adjusted p; adjusted p>0.05 is inconclusive, with the Branch B diagnostic reported as a labelled diagnostic, or as unavailable if D80 or the interval is undefined (H1 section 10); an undefined p or a failure is a not-estimable or failed outcome. If m_E4=0, E4 is not estimable and enters with Holm input 1. The reference is stated as "fixed-date AR(2) surrogates fitted to each episode's first vintage containing the quarter before onset". Wording keeps the limit of SD-07: E4 measures the revision sensitivity of the H1 statistic in the first vintage that contains the quarter before onset; it is not a test of warning available before the onset quarter began, and the onset dates come from final data. Report the signed statistic and limitations even after rejection. The episode-resampling interval (H1 section 7, stream 5405) is a conditional diagnostic, not asserted to have calibrated coverage; the secondary comparisons are prespecified sensitivity evidence, and none replaces the confirmatory comparison. The real-time-against-final and two-clocks descriptions carry no test and are not a claim of real-time warning. See E4 sections 2, 7 and 9-11.
