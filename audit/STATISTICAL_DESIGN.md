## SD-01 — What H1 actually tests

**Original location:** pages 8, 10–11; AT-15 on page 21.

Preserve the primary sample, growth transform, 40-observation rolling fit, recession and merging rule, Delta_r, S, endogenous-onset surrogate construction and registered upper-tail p-value. The inferential statement is:

> The observed mean pre-onset change in the fitted root modulus was unusually large relative to series generated by the registered fitted constant-parameter AR(2) surrogate procedure, conditional on the procedure's model assumptions and calibration.

This is a comparison against a specified model, not a test against every possible process with “no warning.” The full-sample fitted coefficients and resampled residual distribution define the plug-in reference procedure. Its adequacy must be examined by the planned calibration; the plus-one p-value convention alone is not a demonstration of finite-sample validity for estimated nuisance parameters.

Report S, its sign, the individual Delta_r values, eligible episode count, retained surrogate count, dropped surrogate count, the p-value and its simulation precision. An upper-tail rejection does not logically imply S > 0: a negative S can lie above a more negative surrogate distribution. A count k of m episodes with Delta_r > 0 is descriptive; H1 has not established k individually significant warnings.

The endogenous-onset rule is a substantive strength: it carries the episode-selection rule into the null simulation. Retain it. Selection of time series because they experienced a transition can itself create misleading early-warning patterns; Boettiger and Hastings demonstrate this in their original simulation study. This is motivation for the existing design, not a claim that their model validates H1. [Boettiger & Hastings (2012)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.1204)

Interpretation must remain model-dependent. Dakos and colleagues explicitly discuss surrogate-model dependence and the possibility of false alarms and missed signals. H1 does not independently identify a causal tipping mechanism or prove a useful real-time forecasting strategy. [Dakos et al. (2012), significance testing and discussion](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0041010)

AT-15 is a useful check at the stated synthetic design. It is not evidence of calibration across all constant AR(2) parameters, changing volatility, alternative innovation laws or non-AR(2) dynamics. Preserve the original check and state its scope. Any additional robustness-calibration family should be defined before its official runs, not chosen to rescue a disappointing primary result.

## SD-02 — Branch B: preserve its numbers, limit its conclusion

**Original location:** pages 9–11, H1.1 and section 9.1; AT-16 on page 21.

The proposed comparison combines:

1. A primary statistic whose episodes are selected by the recession rule.
2. An interval formed by resampling the observed eligible episode changes.
3. A D80 estimated from power at five externally fixed dates in planted-signal simulations.

The original plan explicitly calls the fixed-date design a proxy for the primary test. There is no established identity between their power functions or inferential targets. A detectable size is also not automatically a substantive threshold for what counts as an economically meaningful rise.

There is a second unresolved issue: the interval's repeated-sampling target is unspecified. The observed historical episodes may be treated as a fixed set, a sample of an episode population or selections within a stochastic time series. Those are different targets. Resampling a few observed Delta_r values does not by itself establish coverage for the selected-time-series target. The 8-quarter merging rule does not prevent different episodes' 40-quarter estimation windows from overlapping. This concern follows from the displayed window geometry; it is not a claim that dependence was empirically measured.

**Conservative default adopted for drafting:** keep the original S, 90% episode-percentile interval and D80 calculations as explicitly labelled diagnostics. When p > 0.05, use:

> The primary comparison did not detect an unusually large pre-onset change under the registered surrogate model. The interval and fixed-date detectability diagnostic are reported, but they do not establish the absence of a sizeable rise.

This supersedes the proposed “The economy did not approach the boundary before it fell” wording. The primary conclusion remains inconclusive even when the diagnostic interval lies below D80, unless an inferential justification is completed and registered first. If D80 is undefined, state that directly.

**Possible inferential repair, not yet adopted:** define a target parameter and an effect threshold, construct an upper confidence bound with justified coverage under the same episode-selection design, and calibrate the resulting one-sided exclusion rule. A two-sided equivalence claim would require both bounds. The general distinction between non-significance and excluding meaningful effects is supported by Lakens; his t-test examples are not a ready-made solution for this time-series setting. A design-based detectability bound could be considered, but its target and calibration would still have to match. [Lakens (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177)

Before any official power run, specify the exact interpolation rule for D80: interpolation of kappa, mean S and rejection probability; handling of nonmonotonicity within Monte Carlo uncertainty; and whether the reported diagnostic includes uncertainty. Do not enforce a smooth increasing curve by discarding inconvenient simulated outcomes.

## SD-03 — Correct the Wald normalization

**Original location:** page 14, C3.T(a–b) and C3.TEST; page 22 formula sheet.

The PDF states:

- sqrt(n) (phi_hat - phi) converges to N(0, Sigma).
- D = phi_1^2 + 4 phi_2, with gradient d = (2 phi_1, 4).
- sigma_D^2 = d' Sigma d.

Consequently, directly from those displayed statements:

- sqrt(n) (D_hat - D) converges to N(0, sigma_D^2).
- The approximate standard error of D_hat is sigma_D / sqrt(n).
- At D = 0, the standardized candidate is Z_D = sqrt(n) D_hat / sigma_D_hat.
- For H0: D >= 0 versus H1: D < 0, reject when Z_D < -z_(1-alpha), using an explicitly defined upper normal quantile.

The printed D_hat / sigma_D_hat is therefore missing sqrt(n) if sigma_D_hat estimates the asymptotic standard deviation defined earlier. It is correct only if its denominator is separately defined as the finite-sample standard error. Adopt distinct names in code and prose, such as asymptotic_sd and standard_error.

This correction does not prove the candidate has adequate small-sample size. Specify whether the finite-sample normalization uses the stored series length n or the number of regression rows n-2. They are asymptotically equivalent but can affect the proposed small-sample comparisons.

For the spectral criterion G = -4 phi_2 - |phi_1| (1-phi_2), G > 0 is the alternative. Away from phi_1 = 0,

- grad G = (-sign(phi_1)(1-phi_2), -4+|phi_1|).
- Its Wald candidate has the positive rejection direction: Z_G > z_(1-alpha).
- At phi_1 = 0 this gradient is undefined. The kink cannot be handled by silently substituting sign(0)=0 and claiming the same ordinary delta-method result.

All statements in this section are algebraic consequences of the plan, not results from a new simulation.

## SD-04 — Composite null and boundary projection

**Original location:** page 14, C3.TEST.

The two no-cycle nulls within the stationarity triangle are composite: D >= 0 and G <= 0 respectively. A covariance-metric projection selects one fitted boundary point. That construction alone does not establish that the point maximizes rejection probability over the null, even if the boundary is the natural place to investigate.

Adopt “boundary-projection parametric bootstrap candidate.” Before a stronger validity claim, specify the projection objective, feasible set, covariance estimate and point at which it is evaluated, optimizer and tolerances, ties/nonunique projections, unstable starting estimates, and behavior at the spectral kink. Then provide appropriate theory or label the evidence as restricted simulation calibration.

Berger and Boos describe nuisance-parameter p-values based on maximization and confidence-set restriction. This supports the distinction between a plug-in point and a nuisance-aware construction; their paper does not validate the proposed AR(2) projection. A supremum or confidence-set method is an option for later investigation, not an adopted replacement. [Berger & Boos (1994), publisher abstract](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476836)

Keep DR-3's existing grid and tolerance unless changed before registration. Describe a successful candidate as “meeting the preregistered calibration tolerance on the tested grid.” A grid check does not prove uniform validity between its points or under other innovation laws. A conservative test with size below 0.035 would fail DR-3's chosen tolerance even though small size alone is not a violation of Type I error control; the terminology should make this distinction clear.

## SD-05 — Registration conventions to complete

**Original location:** page 14; H1 synthetic specification on pages 9–10; acceptance tests on pages 21–22.

These are audit-identified missing or insufficiently precise fields, not silently adopted design changes:

| Topic | Required pre-registration specification |
|---|---|
| Generating law | Innovation distribution, variance, intercept/mean, independence assumptions and any robustness distributions. Gaussian, zero-mean, unit-variance C3 innovations are a possible baseline, not yet a frozen choice. |
| Initialization | Exact stationary initialization or stated initial values and burn-in. Do not let library defaults decide. |
| Sample size | Distinguish stored observations, regression rows and the n used in bias corrections and Wald standard errors. |
| Estimators | Exact LS and Yule–Walker conventions; source and finite-sample convention for the bias correction; treatment of intercepts and demeaning. |
| Alternatives | Exact coordinates for both cycle definitions, which boundary variance sets the displacement, and deterministic checks of stationarity. |
| Numerical failures | Rank deficiency, nonfinite values, unstable estimates, nonpositive estimated variances, failed projections and bootstrap failures. Record every attempted replicate and failure; preregister how each contributes to performance measures. |
| Bootstrap | Innovation law, residual treatment where applicable, initialization, refitting, bootstrap replicate count, p-value and ties, and RNG streams. Specify which uncertainty the bootstrap includes. |
| Warp-speed screening | Exact pooled construction, its supported interpretation, and which finalists receive full bootstrap validation. One draw per outer replication is not a one-draw substitute for a usable data-analysis p-value. |
| Selection | Explicit size and power summaries, equal-weight averaging rule, tie-breaking, Monte Carlo uncertainty and the no-candidate branch. |
| Reproducibility | Seed derivation per cell and stage, parallel-stream independence, saved draws versus summaries, software versions and rerun policy. |

The simulation reporting framework should state aims, data-generating mechanisms, estimands or testing targets, methods and performance measures. Report Monte Carlo uncertainty and distinguish verification in a few settings from robustness evaluation. Morris, White and Crowther provide the primary methodological basis for these general requirements; the AR(2)-specific entries above are this audit's recommendations. [Morris, White & Crowther (2019), sections 3–5](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8086)

For orientation only, the binomial Monte Carlo standard error sqrt(p(1-p)/R) is approximately 0.00218 at p=0.05 and R=10,000, and 0.0154 at p=0.05 and R=200. These are analytic calculations, not simulation results. Report uncertainty near acceptance thresholds rather than treating every estimated rate as exact.

The cited warp-speed paper establishes properties under stated conditions; implementation must be matched to the relevant construction before that result is claimed. Full-bootstrap finalist validation remains part of the original plan. [Giacomini, Politis & White (2013), publisher abstract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/econometric-theory/article/abs/warpspeed-method-for-conducting-monte-carlo-experiments-involving-bootstrap-estimators/1AAC5F31AB01CE60405A8B4D3FF90D4C)

An independent set of simulation random streams for validating a selected finalist is recommended to separate candidate selection from its reported confirmation. This is an audit recommendation requiring a pre-registration budget and rule; it is not an unrecorded expansion of the grid or permission to rerun until a candidate passes.

## SD-06 — When no test qualifies

**Original location:** page 14, DR-3 and C3.TEST completion criterion.

Adopt this explicit fallback:

> If no candidate meets DR-3, report that no candidate satisfied the registered calibration criterion. Publish the comparison and its uncertainty without recommending a qualifying test.

Do not select the least bad candidate and call it valid. Subsequent methodological work can propose and separately register a revised candidate family. Preserve the original results and clearly distinguish development from confirmation.

For C3.APP and the later package, classification functions and exploratory test outputs can still be documented. They must not be presented as the missing validated test. The corresponding “recommended test implemented” deliverable remains unmet unless the programme records a justified scope revision.

## SD-07 — E4 requires two clocks

**Original location:** page 12, E4.

The specification selects the first vintage containing quarter t_r-1. That quarter's reference period ends before t_r, but the vintage can be released during t_r. The ONS methodology describes first quarterly estimates as published around six weeks after the reference period; this is documentation, not verification of every historical release date. [ONS GDP QMI, timeliness and dissemination](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/grossdomesticproductgdp/methodologies/grossdomesticproductgdpqmi)

Retain the vintage comparison, but separate:

- The quarter whose activity is measured.
- The actual release date and timestamp of the vintage.
- The date on which the proposed signal could have been computed.
- The onset's reference quarter and the later date at which the recession rule could be observed.

Before E4 registration, verify source coverage using metadata and freeze release-date matching, missing-vintage treatment, and the information set used for each decision. If the goal is strict warning before the onset quarter starts, a first vintage of t_r-1 may be too late; retaining the original vintage rule supports a historical revision-sensitivity exercise with explicitly measured availability, not an automatic pre-onset operational warning claim. Do not substitute a new forecasting design without documenting that choice.
